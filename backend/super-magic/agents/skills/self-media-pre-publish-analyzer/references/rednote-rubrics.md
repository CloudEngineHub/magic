Use this file for Xiaohongshu/Rednote image-card posts. Score the current work from the mentioned post folder. Do not score from title or chat context alone.

## Scoring Anchors

- 90-100: publishable with a clear competitive edge; only polish copy or minor visual details.
- 80-89: publishable after targeted edits; at least one major dimension is below its goal requirement.
- 70-79: recognizable idea but weak first-screen, proof, rhythm, or CTA; revise before publishing.
- 60-69: likely underperforms; needs structural rewrite or card sequence redesign.
- Below 60: incomplete or mismatched; diagnose missing files and recommend rebuilding from brief.

For each dimension, give sub-scores. A sub-dimension earns full credit only when there is visible evidence in the files. If evidence is absent, say what file or card is missing.

## Goal Scenario: IP增长

Judge whether the post strengthens the creator/IP as a durable content asset.

High-priority evidence:

- The cover/title express a recognizable point of view, not only a generic tip.
- The first two cards quickly answer: who is speaking, why should I trust them, and why follow this account.
- Advice is consistent with the creator's role, product, brand voice, and prior promise in metadata.
- The post can become a series: repeatable format, named method, recurring problem, or signature checklist.
- The ending gives a follow reason stronger than a generic "follow for more".

Common deductions:

- Useful information but no creator viewpoint.
- Persona conflicts with the product or brand promise.
- The reader can save the post but has no reason to remember the account.
- The content sounds like commodity SEO or generic AI output.

Recommendation priority:

1. Strengthen viewpoint and creator authority.
2. Add proof, story, role context, or named method.
3. Make the ending point toward a followable series.
4. Tune cover/title only after the identity promise is clear.

## Goal Scenario: 产品转化

Judge whether the post creates qualified interest, not just likes.

High-priority evidence:

- The topic maps to a real buying/consulting pain and a specific audience state.
- The product/service appears as a natural solution, not a sudden advertisement.
- Cards show proof: case, before/after, process, result, comparison, or mechanism.
- Objections are handled before CTA: price, difficulty, risk, credibility, timing.
- CTA is concrete: comment keyword, consultation path, next step, or decision criterion.

Common deductions:

- Pain point is broad but the product value is vague.
- Benefits are asserted without evidence or mechanism.
- CTA appears only at the end and is disconnected from the argument.
- The post attracts the wrong audience for conversion.

Recommendation priority:

1. Clarify the target user's urgent pain.
2. Tie each core claim to proof or mechanism.
3. Insert objection handling before the CTA.
4. Rewrite the CTA as a low-friction next action.

## Goal Scenario: 爆文流量

Judge whether the post has click, completion, search, and interaction potential.

High-priority evidence:

- Cover and title create a clear information gap or outcome promise within one glance.
- Keywords match how users search the topic, not just how the creator describes it.
- First card delivers immediate stakes, contrast, or surprise.
- The card sequence has fast scan rhythm and frequent payoff points.
- The ending creates a comment, save, share, or debate trigger that fits the topic.

Common deductions:

- Cover has too much text or weak visual hierarchy.
- Title is accurate but not clickable.
- Cards are visually polished but the first three seconds are flat.
- No strong reason to comment, save, or share.

Recommendation priority:

1. Fix cover/title promise and keyword alignment.
2. Move the strongest contrast or payoff to card 1.
3. Compress repeated cards and add save-worthy structure.
4. Add one specific interaction trigger.

## Red Flags

- Medical, financial, legal, or safety claims without evidence or caveats.
- False scarcity, fake guarantees, or exaggerated results.
- Cover/title bait that the cards do not deliver.
- Third-party platform advice treated as official policy.
