# WeChat Official Account Rubrics

Use this file for WeChat Official Account articles. Score the article from the mentioned post folder, including `post.json`, article HTML, cover assets, summary, and publishing metadata when available.

## Base Scorecard

Total: 100. Keep these weights for all goals.

| Dimension | Weight | Sub-dimensions |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Title, summary, and cover open-rate potential | 20 | Title promise 6; title specificity 4; summary value preview 4; cover-title fit 3; audience clarity 3 |
| Long-form structure and completion experience | 25 | Lead hook 5; problem-to-solution arc 6; section hierarchy 5; transition logic 4; ending momentum 5 |
| Information gain, credibility, and supporting cases | 20 | Original insight 5; evidence/cases 5; mechanism explanation 4; data/source handling 3; counterargument or boundary 3 |
| Mobile formatting and reading load | 20 | Paragraph length 4; heading density 4; visual rhythm 4; emphasis discipline 3; table/image readability 3; mobile CTA placement 2 |
| Conversion action, follow reason, and brand relationship | 15 | CTA clarity 4; follow reason 3; brand/IP consistency 3; objection handling 3; next-step friction 2 |

## Scoring Anchors

- 90-100: strong opening, credible substance, smooth mobile reading, and a clear reason to follow or act.
- 80-89: publishable after focused edits; one key area weakens open rate, completion, or conversion.
- 70-79: concept is usable but the article asks too much of readers before delivering payoff.
- 60-69: structure, evidence, or formatting blocks completion; revise before publishing.
- Below 60: missing article/cover/summary or unclear value proposition; rebuild before publishing.

For each dimension, show sub-scores and cite evidence from title, summary, cover, sections, paragraphs, images, or CTA. Missing assets should reduce score and confidence.

## Goal Scenario: IP增长

Judge whether the article builds durable trust and a memorable account relationship.

High-priority evidence:

- The title and lead communicate a point of view the account can own repeatedly.
- The article shows stable expertise: cases, framework, principles, operating details, or lived experience.
- The author/IP voice is visible without becoming self-indulgent.
- The ending gives a specific follow reason: series, framework, resource, future topic, or ongoing perspective.
- Brand/product mentions support trust rather than interrupting the reading experience.

Common deductions:

- Article is informative but anonymous; any account could have written it.
- Long intro delays the author's strongest insight.
- Trust is asserted through adjectives instead of proof.
- The follow reason is generic or absent.

Recommendation priority:

1. Move the author's distinctive thesis into title/lead.
2. Add proof and first-person operating evidence where claims are broad.
3. Shape sections into a repeatable framework or named series.
4. Replace generic follow copy with a specific account promise.

## Goal Scenario: 产品转化

Judge whether the article can produce qualified consultation, trial, purchase, or sales interest.

High-priority evidence:

- Title/summary identify a buyer-relevant pain or decision moment.
- The article makes the problem expensive, urgent, or strategically important before selling.
- Product value is explained through mechanism, contrast, case, or workflow.
- Objections are anticipated: cost, implementation burden, risk, alternatives, proof, fit.
- CTA appears after sufficient trust and names a concrete next action.

Common deductions:

- Product appears before the reader accepts the problem.
- Case/evidence is too thin for the claim.
- CTA is vague, high-friction, or detached from the article's argument.
- The article converts fans poorly because it optimizes only for reading.

Recommendation priority:

1. Rebuild the problem-to-solution arc.
2. Add evidence near every product claim.
3. Insert objection handling before CTA.
4. Make CTA specific, low-friction, and measurable.

## Goal Scenario: 爆文流量

Judge whether the article can win opens, completion, forwarding, and discussion.

High-priority evidence:

- Title/summary create tension, specificity, or timely relevance without misleading.
- Cover reinforces the opening promise and is legible on mobile.
- Lead delivers payoff quickly enough to prevent early drop-off.
- Sections are skimmable and have a clear forward motion.
- The article has a social forwarding reason: identity, utility, debate, warning, checklist, or timely reference.

Common deductions:

- Title is literary but not specific.
- Summary repeats title instead of adding a reason to open.
- Article buries the strongest point after too much setup.
- Formatting is dense on mobile, lowering completion.
- No clear reason to forward or discuss.

Recommendation priority:

1. Improve title/summary/cover as one opening package.
2. Move the strongest payoff into the first screen.
3. Tighten section rhythm and paragraph length.
4. Add a forwardable asset: list, framework, chart, quote, or stance.

## Red Flags

- Overclaiming expertise, outcomes, revenue, health, finance, or legal effects.
- Dense mobile paragraphs, unreadable tables, or images that carry essential text.
- CTA before trust is established.
- Misleading title/cover that the article does not deliver.
- Third-party layout or cover tips treated as official WeChat platform rules.
