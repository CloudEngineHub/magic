Bottom line

Worth doing only if onboarding changes Decision: whether to redesign the first-week onboarding.

Week-two churn is driven less by missing features than by the absence of a first "win" in week one.[Q1][S1]

  1. Users who never tie the app to a recurring task forget it once the novelty fades.[Q2]
  2. Peers, not feature lists, are what make owner-operators try again.[S2]

Business recommendations

Target segment priority

Start with owner-operators who already run one recurring weekly task on paper.

Hypotheses: H1 · Evidence: [Q1][S1] · Validate first: which weekly task they would move first.

One-line positioning

"Your weekly routine, done in five minutes."

Hypotheses: H1 · Validate first: whether real users describe the job in these words.

Pricing direction

A low monthly tier in the range of 30-60 per month, with annual billing as an option.

AI respondents are optimistic about price. Use this as a direction only, not to set a price, until real customers confirm it.

Hypotheses: H2 · Validate first: willingness to pay after a two-week trial.

Channels and reach

Referrals from existing users in the same trade, plus supplier partnerships.

Hypotheses: H3 · Evidence: [S2] · Validate first: who they asked last time they adopted a tool.

First 30 days

  1. Run the validation questionnaire with 8-15 current trial users.
  2. Prototype a three-day onboarding that ends with one recurring task set up.

Hypotheses: H1, H2 · Validate first: H1 with real trial users.

Trust comes from peers, not features

Medium confidence

Most owner-operators delay adoption until someone they know uses the product; feature lists did not move them.[S2]

  • Owner-operators: most support; peer proof is the trigger to try.
  • Chain managers: a few mention it; a free trial matters more.

Counter-evidence: one efficiency-driven owner adopted after a free trial without peer proof.[Q4] Related: H3.

Assumptions and limitations

  • Respondents are AI-simulated personas, not real users. Their answers reflect the profiles and the model's general knowledge.
  • Effective sample: 7 completed interviews out of 8 planned; 1 interview failed (persona profile incomplete).
  • Social media evidence was collected through search engines only and does not represent full platform coverage.
  • Persona traits marked "(inferred)" in transcripts are model assumptions, not sourced facts. 1 interview with a high share of inferred answers was downweighted.
  • AI samples do not support statistical inference; "most", "some", and "a few" describe the AI interviews only.
  • 2 quotes that could not be matched word for word in the transcripts were removed during the quote self-check.
